Imagine digging a hole straight through the center of the Earth, then jumping in. How long until you pop out the other side?

First, the digging. Earth is almost eight thousand miles across. The deepest hole humans have ever drilled, the Kola Superdeep Borehole in Russia, reached about seven and a half miles. It took nearly two decades, and it never made it through the crust.

Below the crust waits the mantle, about eighteen hundred miles of hot rock that slowly flows. Then a liquid metal outer core. And at the center, a solid ball of iron and nickel at up to nine thousand eight hundred degrees Fahrenheit, about as hot as the Sun's surface.

So let's cheat. You get an indestructible tube with all the air pumped out. You step off the edge, and you fall. At first it feels like any other fall, just one that keeps going.

Here's the surprise. As you sink, gravity doesn't weaken right away. It actually gets stronger, peaking about nine percent above normal near the core. The rock above you pulls in all directions and cancels out, while the dense iron center keeps pulling you down.

Then gravity fades, reaching zero at the very center. By now you're moving faster than eighteen thousand miles per hour, faster than the International Space Station orbits the planet.

You shoot through the center, and now gravity works against you, slowing you down just as much as it sped you up. You rise and rise, and right as you reach the far side, you stop. Grab the edge, or you fall all the way back.

Total travel time? The classic textbook answer is about forty two minutes. But in twenty fifteen, physicist Alexander Klotz used real data on Earth's layers and got about thirty eight minutes. Shorter than a lot of commutes.

Of course, reality ruins the ride. With air in the tube, drag would cap your speed near one hundred twenty miles per hour, and you'd end up bobbing to a stop at the center. And Earth's spin would shove you into the wall, unless your tunnel runs pole to pole.

One last twist. Drop in from most of the United States, and the far side of the planet is the Indian Ocean. So pack a swimsuit.
