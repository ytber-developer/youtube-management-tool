What if you fell into a black hole? Let's find out. And let's hope you packed light.

A black hole is matter crushed so tightly that its gravity traps everything, even light. Its edge is called the event horizon. Cross it, and there is no way back.

Fall into a small black hole, a few times the mass of our Sun, and gravity pulls your feet far harder than your head. You get stretched like a noodle. Scientists really call this spaghettification.

But pick a giant, like Sagittarius A star at the center of our galaxy, four million times the mass of the Sun, and the pull is gentle at the edge. You would drift across the horizon and feel nothing at all.

To a friend watching from far away, you would never quite make it. Your image would slow down, turn red, and fade, frozen at the edge.

For you, the fall goes on, toward a point where our laws of physics simply break down. What happens there? Nobody knows yet.
