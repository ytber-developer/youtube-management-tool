Tonight, look up at the Moon. Now imagine it simply blinks out of existence. What would actually change down here?

The first thing you'd notice is the dark. Every night would be as black as a new Moon night. You'd see more stars, but creatures that time their lives by moonlight, from spawning corals to night hunters, would lose their clock.

Next, the oceans. The Moon's gravity pulls Earth's water into bulges, and those bulges are your tides. The Sun makes tides too, but its pull is a little less than half the Moon's. Without the Moon, tides would shrink to roughly a third of today's size.

Here's something strange. Earth and the Moon actually circle a shared balance point, about a thousand miles beneath your feet. So Earth wobbles a little every month. Erase the Moon, and that wobble stops. Our orbit around the Sun would barely change.

The biggest change is slow. Earth's axis leans about twenty three and a half degrees, and over tens of thousands of years it only nods between roughly twenty two and twenty four and a half. The Moon's pull helps keep it that steady.

Mars has no big moon, and its tilt swings far more. Without ours, scientists think Earth's tilt could drift much further too, making seasons wilder over millions of years. Not in your lifetime, but on the timescales that shape climate and life.

And your days? The Moon's tides act like a brake, stretching each day by a tiny fraction of a second every century. Lose the Moon, and most of that braking stops. That pale rock in your sky is quietly shaping time itself.
