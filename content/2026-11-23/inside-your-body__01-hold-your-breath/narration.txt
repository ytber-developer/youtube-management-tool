Take a deep breath and hold it. Right now, your body is already starting a countdown, and the alarm it's waiting for might surprise you.

Your lungs hold enough oxygen to keep you going for about four minutes. Yet most people give up after roughly one. So if you're not out of oxygen, what makes you quit?

Every second, your cells keep making carbon dioxide, and it builds up in your blood. Meanwhile, your breathing rhythm never stops. You're fighting it by holding your diaphragm still, and that strained muscle keeps sending louder and louder signals.

Here's a twist. Dip your face in water while you hold your breath, and your heart slows down. It's called the diving response, a reflex thought to help your body save oxygen.

Finally, you hit the breaking point. Your automatic rhythm wins, and air rushes back in. On dry land, for healthy adults, that happens long before oxygen runs dangerously low.
